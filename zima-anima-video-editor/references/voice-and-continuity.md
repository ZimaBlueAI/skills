# Voice, dialogue and continuity

## ASR / STT and captions

Check the available speech-to-text engine, language, file accessibility and confidentiality before processing. Preserve original audio. Transcribe with word or segment timing when supported; label uncertain words, names and overlapping speakers. Listen to relevant original audio and correct the transcript; never present guessed words as exact. Align captions to the final edit, not the original transcript alone. Verify readable duration, line breaks, speaker changes, pronunciation and subtitle language against the rendered video. Record engine, language, source file and unreviewed segments.

## Voiceover / TTS and breathing

Identify the exact approved script, language, intended voice and target duration. Break it into sense groups and mark natural breath opportunities. Direct relative volume, speed, pauses and intonation for each phrase; distinguish acting instructions from text to be spoken. Select a provider that actually supports the desired language, voice and controls; do not invent provider tags or promise exact timing. Generate a short audition, listen for mispronunciation, emotion, clipping and artifacts, then revise and render the selected take. Fit the final take to the edit by revising the wording only with the user's authorization or adjusting timing where appropriate; avoid degrading speech by extreme retiming. Preserve the audio and script version and recheck subtitles.

Voice cloning requires the necessary permission from the voice owner; avoid impersonation or misrepresentation. For local, cloud and paid engines, check configuration and network/fee implications before use. Without a suitable engine, return a script and voice-direction sheet and say synthesis was not performed.

## Dialogue and lip sync

For existing speech, retain the original timing and room tone unless an authorized edit calls for replacement. For replacement dialogue, lock the audio take before attempting lip sync. Record source picture, approved audio and affected range. Choose an actually available lip-sync tool or manual edit route; a transcription match, waveform alignment or successful render alone does not establish visible sync. Watch the real mouth and listen around phrase beginnings, closures, pauses and endings at normal speed and frame-by-frame where needed; note occlusion, side profiles, multiple speakers and uncertainty. Recheck whenever picture or audio changes. If no lip-sync tool is available, preserve the original or provide a repair plan instead of claiming success.

## Character identity and camera

Collect user-approved face, body, hairstyle, clothing and proportion references where provided; record shot and source times. Compare visible features through the moving passage, including start, middle, end and meaningful changes of pose or scale. Separate identity errors from lens perspective, lighting, expression or intended change. A localized repair should preserve the surrounding performance, framing and audio; record the original and repaired version. For wider mismatches prepare a full-shot replacement brief and maintain the continuity context. Face replacement and generative repair need a compatible tool, consent where applicable and actual visual review; a still-sheet comparison or prompt cannot verify motion.

Editing can choose and reframe existing camera movement, but cannot create a physically correct new move by instruction alone. For a requested synthetic move or changed shot, identify the generation/compositing tool and references, output a concrete brief if unavailable, and inspect the resulting video for background stability and subject motion before claiming success.
